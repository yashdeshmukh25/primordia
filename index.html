What is this

The one rule — every species is just a number grid. Cell (i, j) says how strongly species i is pulled toward or pushed away from species j at short range. That grid is the entire design.

Three layers now — local chemistry (short-range, particle to particle), deep field (long-range, species center-of-mass to center-of-mass — whole swarms can chase or flee each other), and bonds (compatible particles physically link, stretch, and snap). None of them know about each other. What you see is all three running at once.

Autoevolve — turn on Drift and the local rule grid mutates itself every couple of seconds, on its own, forever. The organism you're watching is not the one you started with ten minutes ago.

Life and death — a fourth, optional layer. Particles spend energy to move, earn a trickle back for free, split in two past a threshold (the child can randomly mutate to a different species), and vanish at zero. Turn it on and population becomes an outcome, not a setting — species can boom, starve, or go extinct while you watch.

Why it's shown this way — the same pattern — simple local rule, no center, real complexity — runs underneath flocking birds, immune cells, and galaxy clusters. Nobody designs the flock. The rule is enough; these are just more rules, stacked.
